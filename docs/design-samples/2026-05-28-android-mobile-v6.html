§ 01

The brief.

Two pages · intent

An Arduino IDE for the phone is a strange thing to want. Cables are for desks, phones are for elsewhere — which is the point. The bench is wherever the board is: a vehicle bay, a hangar floor, the back of a Land Cruiser on the perimeter. The IDE follows.

The brief is small. Five screens, one verb each. Sketch. Edit. Probe. Pick. Observe. The rest is plumbing — keep the chassis quiet so the operator can hear the board.

This document fixes the visual form. Sentient for editorial moments, Switzer for body — both Pangram Pangram, both quiet. The chassis is warm graphite, the ink is warm cream, and there is no other colour. Status is communicated by inversion, not by hue.

What is hidden is as important as what is shown. No gradients, no grain, no atmosphere. Restraint is the brand. Add nothing. Defend everything that remains.

01/Sketchbook · entry surface

Sketches,
at rest.

The landing surface. A breathing dot for the linked board, recent work below grouped by day, the primary verb sitting on a hairline at the foot of the screen. One verb. No FAB.

01
Breathing dot, not icon
Connection state is a single dot that breathes when linked. No badge, no chip, no green text — the dot is the signal.
02
Sketch names in italic serif
Names get editorial weight; metadata stays in mono. The contrast tells you what's content and what's data without thinking.
03
"New sketch" is a row, not a button
A hairline rule with the verb and an open circle. Reads as continuation, not interruption.

02/Editor · core surface

Edit,
then flash.

The primary surface. Tabs, gutter, source. Pin literals get a one-pixel hairline ring; the active line inverts; the flash verb sits in italic serif at the foot. One screen, one verb.

01
Pin literal · auto-recognised
Numbers matching the connected board's pinmap get a hairline ring. Tap to open Probe.
02
Active line · full inversion
Cursor row becomes a cream block with dark text. Replaces every selection ring from the desktop with one move.
03
"Flash" is a verb, italic
Serif italic at the bottom of the screen. No color, no fill. The arrow next to it is the only filled shape.
03/Probe · live pinout

The board,
under glass.

Tap a pin literal in the editor and Probe opens to the live pinout. The selected pin breathes cream against the warm chassis; the name appears in serif below; the function in body sans. Hardware-aware, type-led.

01
Real PCB silhouette, not a diagram
A small render of the ForgeBoard chassis with the MCU and pin headers. Reads as the physical object, not as an abstraction.
02
Pin pulses by opacity
No color glow. The cream marker breathes at 2s — calm, alive, monochrome.

04/Boards · select transport

Pick a
board.

USB and WiFi share one surface. The selected device inverts — full cream block, dark text. No checkmark, no badge, no chip. The row itself is the indicator.

01
Selection · inverted row
The whole row becomes the selected state. No little checkmark to track — the inverted block does the work.
02
Identifiers in mono, not "Device 3"
VID:PID, USB path, IP — the operator sees the real address. No translation layer between human and machine.
05/Monitor · live serial

Listen,
then write.

Serial telemetry, time-stamped, level-coded. Levels carry weight rather than colour — INF regular, DBG muted, WRN italic. The cursor breathes at the tail of the most recent line. Reading is primary.

01
Level by typographic weight
INF is regular, DBG is muted, WRN is italic. The level is encoded in the typeface itself — no traffic-light row.
02
Cursor breathes, doesn't blink
Opacity oscillation at 1.2 s — alive without demanding attention. Calmer than a hard blink.
