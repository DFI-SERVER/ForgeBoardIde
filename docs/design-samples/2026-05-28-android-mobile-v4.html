§ Direction Intent
Voice
Restraint

An Arduino IDE for the phone is a strange thing to want. Cables are for desks, and phones are for elsewhere — which is the whole point. The bench is wherever the board is. A vehicle bay, a hangar floor, the back of a Land Cruiser at the perimeter. The IDE follows.

The brief is small. Five screens. One verb each. Sketch. Edit. Flash. Probe. Observe. The rest is plumbing. The trick is the same as it was on the desktop — keep the chassis quiet so the operator can hear the board.

This document fixes the form. Fraunces, variable, optical-sized — used in italic where the verb is, used wide and quiet where the body is. Warm graphite chassis, cream ink, a single deep saffron held in reserve for primary intent. Emerald appears only when something is connected.

What is hidden is as important as what is shown. There are no gradients, no grain textures, no atmospheric blur. The page is paper, the screen is its photograph, the type does the work. If the page were ten percent more designed, it would be less so.

R1 · Progress

Progress as horizon, not number.

Compile and flash are indeterminate; their duration is a function of cable length and karma. A thin saffron wave traces the toolbar. Readable in the periphery while the operator reaches for the cable.
R2 · Keyboard

The keys above the keys.

A soft-keyboard accessory bar with the characters one actually writes in C++ — single tap, no holding shift. The shape of the input adapts to the shape of the language. Reused on the serial monitor.
